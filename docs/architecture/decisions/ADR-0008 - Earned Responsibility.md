# ADR-0008 — Earned Responsibility

## Status

Accepted

---

## Context

DiaperScout relies on community contributions to expand and maintain the Atlas.

Different contributors perform different responsibilities.

Examples include:

- submitting observations;
- reviewing evidence;
- maintaining editorial quality;
- operating the platform.

A decision was required regarding how additional responsibilities should be granted.

One approach would assign responsibilities manually or based solely on account age.

Another would automatically promote contributors after reaching predefined activity thresholds.

A third approach recognises sustained stewardship while leaving significant promotions as human decisions.

---

## Decision

DiaperScout adopts an **Earned Responsibility** model.

Contributors gain additional responsibilities through demonstrated stewardship of the Atlas.

Trust is built through the long-term quality of contributions rather than the quantity of activity.

The platform may recommend contributors for increased responsibility based on factors such as:

- consistently accurate observations;
- high-quality evidence;
- successful completion of Scout Tasks;
- constructive participation over time.

Recommendations assist human decision-making.

They do not replace it.

Significant roles, such as Moderator, remain appointments made by existing moderators or administrators.

---

## Consequences

This decision has several important consequences.

### Positive

- Community trust is built gradually through meaningful contribution.
- Greater responsibility reflects demonstrated stewardship.
- The Atlas is protected from automatic promotion based solely on activity.
- Contributors have a clear pathway to greater involvement.
- Human judgement remains part of significant appointments.

### Trade-offs

- Promotion requires ongoing community oversight.
- Contributors may progress at different rates.
- Trust systems require careful tuning over time.

These trade-offs are considered worthwhile because they prioritise long-term quality over short-term activity.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Automatic Promotion

Automatically grant higher roles after reaching predefined contribution thresholds.

Rejected because activity alone does not demonstrate editorial judgement or stewardship.

---

### Manual Appointment Only

Require all promotions to be initiated manually.

Rejected because outstanding contributors may be overlooked and the platform cannot assist moderators in identifying potential candidates.

---

### Reputation as Authority

Allow high reputation scores to grant additional permissions automatically.

Rejected because reputation measures contribution, not necessarily responsibility or editorial judgement.

## Notes

Responsibility is earned.

Authority is delegated.

Neither replaces evidence.

A contributor's trust influences the responsibilities they may be invited to undertake.

It does not alter the editorial process or create alternative pathways into the Atlas.

This decision reinforces the principle that DiaperScout is maintained through stewardship rather than hierarchy.

The objective is to cultivate a knowledgeable and trusted community capable of preserving the Atlas for many years while ensuring that increased responsibility always reflects demonstrated commitment to its long-term quality.
