# Discovery Task System

## Purpose

DiaperScout's Atlas will always contain gaps.

Products may be missing.

Specifications may be incomplete.

Retail availability may be uncertain.

Conflicting observations may require investigation.

The Discovery Task System provides a structured way for DiaperScout to identify these gaps and invite Explorers to help resolve them.

Discovery Tasks turn uncertainty into opportunities for useful community contribution.

They are a workflow mechanism, not a game or ranking system.

---

# Core Principle

DiaperScout should not ask Explorers to contribute simply to generate activity.

A Discovery Task exists because completing it could improve the quality, coverage or usefulness of the Atlas.

The system should therefore prioritise:

1. Knowledge value
2. Evidence quality
3. Geographic or product coverage
4. Uncertainty reduction
5. Community usefulness

Activity volume is not itself a goal.

---

# Discovery Task Lifecycle

A Discovery Task follows this general lifecycle:

```text
Knowledge Gap
      ↓
Task Creation
      ↓
Task Prioritisation
      ↓
Explorer Chooses Task
      ↓
Observation + Evidence
      ↓
Editorial Review
      ↓
Atlas Update
      ↓
Task Completion
```

A task may also be closed without updating the Atlas when evidence is insufficient or the requested information cannot be verified.

---

# Knowledge Gaps

A Knowledge Gap is an identified area where the Atlas lacks useful or sufficiently reliable information.

Examples include:

* A known Product with incomplete specifications
* A Product with conflicting observations
* A Product believed to be available in a region but lacking recent evidence
* A retailer location with uncertain availability
* A historical Product requiring additional documentation
* A regional variation requiring verification
* A missing Product that has been reported by the community

Knowledge Gaps may be identified automatically, editorially or through community contributions.

---

# Discovery Tasks

A Discovery Task represents a specific, actionable Knowledge Gap.

A task should be narrow enough that an Explorer can reasonably understand what evidence would resolve it.

Examples:

> Confirm whether Product X is currently available at Location Y.

> Photograph the packaging of Product X to verify the stated waist range.

> Investigate whether Product X sold in the United Kingdom differs from the documented European version.

> Find evidence supporting or contradicting the reported discontinuation of Product X.

A Discovery Task should explain what is known, what is uncertain and what evidence would be useful.

---

# Task Categories

Discovery Tasks may belong to several categories.

## Availability Confirmation

Determine whether a Product has recently been observed at a particular Location.

The resulting information represents an Observation.

It does not create a guarantee of live stock.

---

## New Product Discovery

Identify a Product that is not currently represented in the Atlas.

Evidence should establish that the Product exists and, where practical, identify:

* Manufacturer
* Brand
* Product name
* Product type
* Region
* Packaging
* Barcode

---

## Conflicting Evidence

Investigate two or more pieces of evidence that appear to contradict one another.

The objective is to understand the reason for the conflict.

Possible explanations include:

* Regional variation
* Product reformulation
* Packaging change
* Different Product Variant
* Incorrect community information
* Measurement differences

---

## Correction Investigation

Investigate a reported error in the Atlas.

The task should identify the disputed information and the evidence required to establish the correct value.

---

## Regional Verification

Investigate differences between Products, packaging, specifications or availability across regions.

Regional information should never be assumed to be identical.

---

## Specification Completion

Gather missing objective information about an existing Product.

Examples include:

* Waist Range
* Product dimensions
* Fastener Type
* Backing Type
* Capacity
* GTIN
* Pack Type

---

# Task Creation

Discovery Tasks may be created by:

* Editorial workflows
* Automated gap detection
* Community observations
* Moderators
* Future Atlas analysis systems

A task should not be created merely because a Product has not received recent community activity.

The system should distinguish between:

> **Missing knowledge**

and:

> **Missing activity**

Only the former is sufficient reason to create a Discovery Task.

---

# Task Prioritisation

Tasks should be prioritised according to the potential value of resolving the underlying Knowledge Gap.

Factors may include:

* Number of Explorers affected
* Importance of the missing information
* Confidence in the underlying Knowledge Gap
* Geographic coverage
* Product popularity
* Evidence age
* Evidence conflict
* Editorial importance

Prioritisation should not be based solely on the number of contributions an Explorer has made.

---

# Explorer Participation

Discovery Tasks are invitations, not obligations.

An Explorer may:

* Accept a task
* Ignore a task
* Abandon a task
* Submit partial evidence
* Submit evidence that does not resolve the task

Explorers should never be penalised for declining or abandoning a task.

---

# Evidence

Completing a Discovery Task requires evidence appropriate to the task.

Evidence may include:

* Photographs
* Packaging
* Barcodes
* Manufacturer documentation
* Measurements
* Retail observations
* Historical documentation
* Other independently verifiable information

The required evidence should be proportional to the importance of the task.

A task should not require unnecessary evidence simply to satisfy a workflow.

---

# Editorial Review

Discovery Tasks do not directly modify the Atlas.

All resulting observations and evidence enter the normal editorial workflow.

```text
Discovery Task
      ↓
Observation
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Editorial Review
      ↓
Atlas
```

Editorial decisions remain authoritative.

A successful task therefore means that useful evidence was submitted, not that the submitted conclusion was automatically accepted.

---

# Community Trust

Community Trust is an internal signal representing an Explorer's demonstrated reliability as a contributor.

It is used to support operational and editorial decisions.

Community Trust may consider factors such as:

* Accuracy of previous contributions
* Evidence quality
* Consistency
* Successful resolution of Knowledge Gaps
* Correction history
* Agreement with independently verified information
* Quality of submitted observations

Completing Discovery Tasks accurately and usefully may contribute positively to Community Trust.

---

# Trust Is Not Reputation

Community Trust is not:

* A public score
* A follower count
* Karma
* XP
* A leaderboard position
* A popularity rating
* A social status indicator

Community Trust should not be presented to other Explorers as a competitive metric.

It exists to help DiaperScout operate a trustworthy community and editorial system.

---

# Trust Is Not Authority

Community Trust does not allow an Explorer to declare information authoritative.

A highly trusted Contributor may produce strong evidence.

That evidence must still pass through the appropriate editorial process.

The principle is:

> **Trust helps evaluate evidence. It does not replace evidence.**

---

# Trust and Workflow

Community Trust may be used internally to support decisions such as:

* Whether a contribution requires additional review
* Whether additional evidence should be requested
* Whether a Contributor is suitable for certain types of community workflow
* Which Discovery Tasks may be appropriate to recommend
* Whether a contribution should receive expedited operational handling

These mechanisms should remain conservative.

Trust should never become an automatic path around editorial review.

---

# Task Completion

A Discovery Task may reach one of several outcomes:

## Resolved

The submitted evidence successfully addressed the Knowledge Gap and resulted in an Atlas update.

## Partially Resolved

Useful information was discovered, but the Knowledge Gap remains incomplete.

## Unresolved

The evidence was insufficient to establish a reliable conclusion.

## Invalid

The underlying task was incorrect, duplicated or no longer relevant.

## Closed

The task cannot reasonably be completed or is no longer useful.

Task completion status should describe the state of the Knowledge Gap rather than reward the Explorer.

---

# Multiple Contributors

A Discovery Task may receive contributions from multiple Explorers.

The first contribution is not automatically the most valuable.

Multiple observations may be necessary to establish confidence.

Where several Explorers contribute useful evidence, each contribution should retain its own provenance.

Community contribution is collaborative rather than competitive.

---

# Discovery Tasks and the Backpack

Meaningful participation in Discovery Tasks may appear in an Explorer's Backpack.

The Backpack may record:

* Notable discoveries
* Completed investigations
* Significant contributions
* Scrapbook recognition
* Contribution history

However, the Backpack should not become a task completion scoreboard.

The purpose is to tell the story of an Explorer's journey.

---

# Discovery Tasks and the Scrapbook

The Scrapbook may recognise particularly meaningful Discovery Task contributions.

Recognition should focus on the significance of the discovery rather than the number of tasks completed.

Examples include:

* Helping document a previously unknown Product
* Resolving an important specification gap
* Establishing evidence for a historical Product
* Expanding coverage into a previously undocumented region
* Resolving a long-standing contradiction

Recognition should feel like preserving expedition memories rather than awarding points.

---

# Automated Task Generation

Future versions of DiaperScout may identify Knowledge Gaps automatically.

Examples include:

* Products with incomplete specifications
* Conflicting observations
* Stale availability evidence
* Regional coverage gaps
* Missing GTINs
* Unverified product reports

Automated systems may suggest Discovery Tasks.

They must not independently modify the Atlas.

All resulting evidence remains subject to the normal editorial workflow.

---

# Abuse Prevention

Discovery Tasks must not become a mechanism for generating artificial engagement.

The system should guard against:

* Duplicate submissions
* Low-quality repeated observations
* Deliberate misinformation
* Manipulated evidence
* Task farming
* Attempts to inflate Community Trust
* Coordinated abuse
* Automated submission

Community Trust should incorporate negative signals where appropriate.

---

# Privacy

Discovery Tasks should reveal only the information necessary to complete the investigation.

Tasks should not expose:

* Private contributor information
* Internal moderation information
* Private trust data
* Sensitive location information
* Internal editorial notes

Public tasks should be written for the community rather than for internal operational systems.

---

# Design Principles

The Discovery Task System follows these principles:

### Useful over active

The objective is improving the Atlas, not increasing activity.

### Evidence over assertion

Tasks are resolved through evidence rather than confidence or popularity.

### Contribution over competition

Multiple Explorers can contribute to the same discovery.

### Trust over reputation

Internal trust helps operate the community but is never presented as social status.

### Editorial authority

Discovery Tasks never bypass editorial review.

### Voluntary participation

Explorers are invited to contribute but are never required to participate.

### Transparency

Significant decisions should retain sufficient provenance to understand how the Atlas changed.

### Privacy

Community participation should not require unnecessary disclosure of personal information.

---

# Summary

The Discovery Task System provides a structured bridge between:

**what DiaperScout does not know**

and

**what the community can help discover.**

It allows the Guide to identify meaningful gaps, invite Explorers to investigate them, preserve the resulting evidence and improve the Atlas through editorial review.

The system exists to make DiaperScout **more useful and more trustworthy**, not merely more active.
