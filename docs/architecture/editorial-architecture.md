# Editorial Architecture

## Purpose

This document describes how DiaperScout transforms evidence into trusted knowledge.

The editorial architecture protects the integrity of the Atlas by ensuring that every significant change follows a consistent, explainable and transparent process.

Rather than allowing contributors to edit canonical knowledge directly, DiaperScout builds the Atlas through editorial judgement informed by evidence.

---

# Philosophy

The Atlas is curated.

It is not crowd-edited.

Community contributions improve the Atlas by providing observations and evidence.

Editors determine how that evidence influences the published Atlas.

The purpose of editorial review is not to slow contribution.

Its purpose is to preserve trust.

---

# Editorial Principles

The editorial architecture should:

- preserve evidence;
- remain transparent;
- encourage contribution;
- reward accuracy;
- maintain consistency;
- strengthen long-term trust.

Editorial review should make correct behaviour easier than incorrect behaviour.

---

# Editorial Responsibility

Editors are custodians of the Atlas.

Their responsibility is to determine the current published understanding of the world.

Editors do not own the truth.

They evaluate the available evidence and publish the most trustworthy interpretation of it.

As new evidence emerges, editorial decisions may change.

The Atlas is therefore expected to evolve over time.

---

# One Editorial Pipeline

Every meaningful change follows the same editorial pathway.

Evidence may originate from:

- Scouts;
- Trusted Scouts;
- Moderators;
- Verified Manufacturers.

The authority of the contributor influences editorial confidence.

It does not create an alternative pathway into the Atlas.

There is only one editorial pipeline.

---

# Editorial Workflow

Every editorial decision follows the same broad lifecycle.

```text
Observation

↓

Evidence

↓

Editorial Review

↓

Decision

↓

Atlas Updated

↓

Editorial History
```

The workflow remains consistent regardless of who submitted the evidence.

---

# Editorial Decisions

Editorial review may result in one of several outcomes.

Examples include:

- Accept
- Reject
- Request Additional Evidence
- Defer Pending Investigation

Every decision should be explainable.

Where practical, editors should reference the evidence that informed the decision.

---

# Editorial Confidence

Not all evidence carries equal weight.

Editorial confidence may be influenced by:

- quality of supporting evidence;
- contributor trust;
- manufacturer documentation;
- corroborating observations;
- previous editorial history.

Editorial confidence assists decision making.

It does not replace editorial judgement.

---

# Canonical Knowledge

Accepted editorial decisions update the Atlas.

The Atlas always represents the best current understanding available.

Previous understanding contributes to editorial history where appropriate.

The Atlas should remain concise.

Editorial history should remain complete.

---

# Provenance

Every significant piece of canonical knowledge should retain provenance.

The architecture should always be capable of answering:

- Why is this believed?
- What evidence supports it?
- Who reviewed it?
- When did it become canonical?
- What changed?

Trustworthy knowledge should always be explainable.

---

# Editorial History

Editorial history records the meaningful evolution of the Atlas.

Examples include:

- accepted corrections;
- revised Product Specifications;
- manufacturer updates;
- regional variation changes.

Immediate operational mistakes corrected through an undo mechanism should not unnecessarily become part of the permanent historical record.

History should explain the evolution of knowledge rather than accidental administration.

---

# Manufacturer Evidence

Verified Manufacturers contribute official observations.

These observations represent highly authoritative evidence.

They do not bypass editorial review.

This preserves a single, consistent editorial architecture regardless of source.

---

# Community Corrections

Every signed-in contributor may submit a correction request.

Correction requests challenge existing Atlas knowledge.

They do not modify canonical information directly.

Instead, they become editorial work supported by evidence.

The objective is continual refinement rather than unrestricted editing.

---

# Moderators

Moderators perform editorial responsibilities on behalf of the community.

Responsibilities include:

- reviewing observations;
- evaluating evidence;
- publishing canonical knowledge;
- maintaining editorial consistency;
- protecting the integrity of the Atlas.

Moderators should explain significant editorial decisions where appropriate.

---

# Promotion

Moderators are not automatically appointed.

The architecture may recommend trusted contributors for promotion based upon:

- sustained high-quality contributions;
- demonstrated stewardship;
- editorial judgement;
- community trust.

Promotion remains a human decision.

Responsibility is earned rather than granted automatically.

---

# Transparency

Editorial review should remain understandable.

Where appropriate, contributors should be able to understand:

- the status of their submission;
- why evidence was accepted;
- why evidence was rejected;
- what additional evidence is required.

Transparency strengthens confidence in both the Atlas and its editorial process.

---

# Architectural Consequences

The editorial architecture produces several important characteristics.

- Contributors improve the Atlas through evidence rather than direct editing.
- Editorial review remains the single pathway into canonical knowledge.
- Every published fact has traceable provenance.
- Manufacturer observations strengthen confidence without bypassing review.
- Editorial history explains how the Atlas evolved.
- Trust is established through transparency rather than authority.

---

# Evolution

The editorial architecture should evolve alongside the Atlas.

Future workflows should continue to preserve the same principles:

- evidence before publication;
- consistent editorial review;
- explainable decisions;
- preserved provenance;
- long-term trust.

The objective is not merely to publish information.

The objective is to build an Atlas whose knowledge can always explain itself.
