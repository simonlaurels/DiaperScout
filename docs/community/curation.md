# Guide Curation

## Purpose

The Guide is intended to become a long-term record of absorbent products and the world around them.

Guide Curation exists to preserve the integrity of that record.

Editors act as custodians of the Guide.

They protect its structure, maintain its factual accuracy and preserve its history so that future Explorers inherit a Guide that is richer than the one that came before.

Editors do not determine truth.

Truth emerges through evidence.

---

# Editorial Philosophy

Editors curate the Guide.

They do not curate the community.

Community knowledge grows through observations.

Editorial work exists to ensure that knowledge is organised, identifiable and preserved.

Whenever possible, the Guide should prefer evidence over editorial judgement.

Editorial intervention should always be the exception rather than the rule.

---

# Stewardship

Editors are responsible for the long-term stewardship of the Guide.

This includes:

- maintaining Product Specifications
- reviewing Candidate Products
- resolving duplicate products
- preserving historical information
- maintaining retailer records
- ensuring structural consistency
- correcting obvious factual errors

Editors should avoid altering Community Observations unless it is necessary to preserve the integrity of the Guide.

---

# Product Specifications

Product Specifications describe products.

They contain factual information obtained from authoritative sources.

Examples include:

- official product names
- manufacturer specifications
- published dimensions
- certifications
- declared materials
- official product imagery (where permitted)

Editors ensure that Product Specifications remain accurate and consistent.

Community Observations never directly modify Product Specifications.

---

# Candidate Products

Every documented product deserves a place within the Guide.

When a Scout discovers an unknown product it initially becomes a Candidate Product.

Candidate Products preserve discoveries while their identity is established.

Editors determine whether a Candidate Product represents:

- a new product
- an existing product
- a product variant
- a packaging revision
- a duplicate discovery

Throughout this process, observations remain attached to the discovery.

No genuine observation should be lost because a product's identity changes.

---

# Community Observations

Community Observations belong to the Scouts who created them.

Editors should not rewrite observations simply because they disagree with them.

Observations become evidence.

Evidence allows the Guide to improve its understanding of the world.

Where editorial correction is necessary it should be:

- transparent
- minimal
- attributable

The original history of an observation should always be preserved wherever practical.

---

# Historical Preservation

The Guide values history.

Discontinued products remain valuable.

Historical packaging remains valuable.

Past retailer observations remain valuable.

Old measurements remain valuable.

Every observation represents what someone genuinely discovered at a particular point in time.

The Guide should preserve those discoveries wherever practical.

History should not disappear simply because the world has changed.

---

# Transparency

Editorial actions should be visible.

Whenever practical, the Guide should preserve:

- who performed an editorial action
- when it occurred
- why it was necessary

Transparency strengthens confidence in the Guide.

Invisible editorial changes should be avoided.

---

# Editorial Restraint

Editors should intervene only where editorial judgement genuinely adds value.

Tasks that can be reliably performed by automation should be automated.

Questions that can be answered through community evidence should be answered through community evidence.

Editorial effort should focus on the areas where careful human judgement is genuinely required.

---

# Relationship with Automation

Automation and editors complement one another.

Automation gathers information that computers can reliably discover.

Editors resolve situations that require understanding, context and judgement.

Neither replaces the community.

Both exist to support it.

---

# Relationship with Scouts

Scouts discover.

Editors curate.

These are different responsibilities.

Scouts expand the Guide by bringing knowledge back from the world.

Editors ensure that knowledge remains organised, identifiable and preserved.

Together they create a Guide that is both trustworthy and continually growing.

---

# Summary

Guide Curation exists to preserve the integrity of the Guide.

Editors protect structure.

Editors preserve history.

Editors maintain factual information.

Editors support the community without replacing it.

The Guide grows through the discoveries of Scouts.

It endures through the stewardship of its Editors.
