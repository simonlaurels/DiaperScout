# Evidence & Trust Model

## Purpose

DiaperScout does not build knowledge from opinions.

It builds knowledge from evidence.

This document describes how observations become evidence, how evidence informs the Guide, and how trust improves the quality of that evidence.

It intentionally does not define algorithms or implementation details.

---

# The Knowledge Model

Knowledge within DiaperScout follows a simple progression.

```text
Scout
    │
Observation
    │
becomes
    ▼
Evidence
    │
interpreted by
    ▼
Guide
    │
presented to
    ▼
Explorer
```

Every concept within this document supports that progression.

---

# Observations

Observations are the smallest unit of community knowledge.

An observation records something experienced by a Scout.

Observations are never considered universal truth.

They describe:

- one person
- one place (where applicable)
- one moment in time

Observations may agree.

Observations may disagree.

Both are valuable.

---

# Evidence

Individual observations become evidence.

Evidence represents the Guide's current understanding of a topic.

Evidence is strengthened through:

- additional observations
- independent confirmation
- diversity of sources
- trusted contributors

Evidence is weakened by:

- contradictory observations
- age
- insufficient data

Evidence naturally evolves as new observations are added.

---

# Trust

Trust exists solely to improve evidence quality.

Trust never determines truth.

Trust never replaces evidence.

Trust simply adds weight to observations.

Trust belongs to Scouts.

It is maintained internally by the Guide.

It is not intended to become a public reputation system.

---

# Confidence

The Guide presents confidence rather than certainty.

Confidence reflects the strength of the available evidence.

Confidence naturally changes as additional observations are received.

Low confidence does not imply incorrect information.

It simply indicates limited evidence.

---

# Independent Confirmation

Independent observations are more valuable than repeated observations from the same Scout.

The Guide values diversity of evidence.

Multiple Scouts independently observing the same outcome provides stronger evidence than repeated observations from a single individual.

---

# Contradictory Evidence

Contradictory observations are expected.

The physical world changes.

Retailers change stock.

Products change.

Packaging changes.

Availability changes.

Contradictory observations should not replace existing evidence.

They should become part of it.

The Guide continually updates its understanding as additional evidence is gathered.

---

# Evidence Summaries

Explorers should not need to read every observation.

The Guide may summarise evidence to communicate its current understanding.

Examples include:

- average measurements
- retailer availability
- common fit observations
- frequently reported characteristics

Summaries should always be traceable back to the underlying observations.

---

# Facts

Facts remain separate from evidence.

Facts originate from authoritative sources.

Evidence originates from Scouts.

Neither replaces the other.

Together they create a richer understanding of the product.

---

# Version History

Observations preserve history.

Corrections should be transparent.

Historical observations remain valuable because they describe what was observed at a particular point in time.

The Guide preserves provenance wherever practical.

---

# Principles

The evidence model is governed by a small number of principles.

- Observations become evidence.
- Trust adds weight to evidence.
- Evidence is challenged by more evidence.
- Facts describe products.
- Observations describe experiences.
- Confidence grows through independent confirmation.
- The Guide presents understanding rather than certainty.

These principles should guide every future decision relating to community knowledge.
