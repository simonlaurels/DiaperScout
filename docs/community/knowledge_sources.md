# Knowledge Sources

## Purpose

The Guide is built from many different sources of knowledge.

No single source can accurately describe every aspect of the world.

DiaperScout deliberately uses the most appropriate source for each type of knowledge.

This document defines those sources and their responsibilities.

---

# Philosophy

Every source of knowledge has strengths.

Every source also has limitations.

The Guide should gather knowledge from the source best able to provide it.

Whenever possible, knowledge should come directly from its origin.

---

# Knowledge Sources

The Guide recognises four primary sources of knowledge.

- Manufacturers
- Scouts
- Automation
- Editors

Each has a distinct responsibility.

---

# Manufacturers

Manufacturers are the authoritative source of factual product information.

Examples include:

- product names
- specifications
- certifications
- declared materials
- official dimensions
- official imagery (where permitted)

Manufacturer information forms the basis of Product Specifications.

Manufacturers do not describe community experience.

---

# Scouts

Scouts are the primary source of Community Observations.

Scouts discover information that only people can reliably observe.

Examples include:

- retailer availability
- product photographs
- pricing
- measurements
- packaging changes
- historical discoveries
- community experiences

Scouts document the world as they find it.

---

# Automation

Automation gathers information that computers can reliably discover.

Automation exists to reduce repetitive work.

Examples include:

- online retailer availability
- online pricing
- manufacturer catalogue imports
- metadata synchronisation
- retailer catalogue information

Automation should not replace genuine community observation.

---

# Editors

Editors preserve the integrity of the Guide.

They organise knowledge rather than create it.

Examples include:

- maintaining Product Specifications
- verifying Candidate Products
- merging duplicate products
- preserving history
- resolving structural inconsistencies

Editors support every other knowledge source.

---

# Shared Knowledge

Some knowledge benefits from multiple sources.

For example:

- Product identity may begin with a Scout discovery and later be verified by an Editor.

- Product Specifications may originate from a Manufacturer and later be maintained by Editors.

- Online retailer information may be gathered through Automation and later confirmed by Scouts.

Each source contributes what it knows best.

---

# Choosing the Right Source

When introducing a new feature, ask:

> Who is the best source of this knowledge?

If the answer is:

A Manufacturer...

...prefer authoritative manufacturer data.

If the answer is:

A Scout...

...prefer Community Observations.

If the answer is:

A computer...

...prefer Automation.

If the answer requires judgement...

...prefer Editors.

This question should guide future design decisions throughout DiaperScout.

---

# Summary

The Guide grows by combining many forms of knowledge.

Manufacturers provide facts.

Scouts provide observations.

Automation gathers machine-discoverable information.

Editors preserve structure and integrity.

Together they create a Guide that is richer, broader and more trustworthy than any single source could produce alone.
