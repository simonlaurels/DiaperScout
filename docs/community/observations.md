# Community Observations

## Purpose

Community Observations are the primary way Scouts contribute knowledge to the Guide.

Every observation records something that a Scout has personally discovered through exploration or careful research.

Observations do not describe universal truth.

They describe what one Scout observed at one moment in time.

Together, Community Observations allow the Guide to build an increasingly accurate understanding of the world.

---

# Philosophy

Community Observations exist to record discoveries.

They are not opinions.

They are not votes.

They are not discussions.

They are evidence.

Every observation contributes another piece to the Guide.

No single observation is expected to tell the whole story.

The Guide becomes more accurate as observations accumulate over time.

---

# Observation Types

DiaperScout recognises multiple forms of observation.

Each contributes a different kind of knowledge.

## Field Research

Field Research represents observations made in the physical world.

Examples include:

- discovering a previously undocumented product
- confirming retailer availability
- photographing products
- recording pricing
- measuring products
- documenting packaging

Field Research should be performed while physically present at the location being documented.

---

## Desk Research

Desk Research represents discoveries made through careful investigation rather than physical exploration.

Examples include:

- manufacturer catalogues
- historical product information
- official documentation
- online retailer information
- archived webpages

Desk Research complements Field Research but does not replace it.

---

# Observation Principles

Every observation should represent:

- something genuinely observed
- one point in time
- one Scout's experience

Observations should describe what was discovered rather than what is believed.

Where uncertainty exists, the observation should accurately reflect that uncertainty.

---

# Ownership

Community Observations belong to the Scout who created them.

Observations remain permanently attributed to their Scout.

Scouts may correct their own observations.

Where editorial correction is required, it should be:

- transparent
- attributable
- minimal

The history of an observation should be preserved wherever practical.

---

# Evidence

Individual observations are valuable.

Many observations become evidence.

The Guide does not ask whether an observation is correct.

Instead it asks:

> What does the available evidence suggest?

Observations naturally gain or lose influence as additional evidence is collected.

Conflicting observations strengthen the Guide's understanding rather than weaken it.

---

# Observation Quality

The Guide encourages high-quality observations.

Whenever practical, observations should be supported by objective evidence.

Examples include:

- barcode scans
- photographs
- retailer selection
- timestamps
- measurements

The Guide should assist Scouts in producing high-quality observations by capturing as much information automatically as possible.

Good observations should also be the easiest observations to create.

---

# Community Behaviour

Observations are never challenged through popularity.

They are never voted into or out of existence.

If a Scout disagrees with an observation, the preferred response is another observation.

Evidence grows through continued exploration rather than debate.

---

# Historical Value

Observations remain valuable even after the world changes.

Products become discontinued.

Packaging changes.

Retailers stop stocking products.

Prices change.

Historical observations preserve the history of those discoveries.

The Guide should preserve that history wherever practical.

---

# Relationship with the Guide

Community Observations improve the Guide.

They do not directly modify Product Specifications.

Instead they become part of the evidence used by the Guide to present its understanding of the world.

Facts describe products.

Observations describe experiences.

Both are essential.

---

# Summary

Every observation is a discovery.

Every discovery becomes evidence.

Every piece of evidence helps the Guide better understand the world.

The Guide grows because Scouts continue bringing knowledge back from the world.
