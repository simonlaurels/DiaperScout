# Scout Pins

## Purpose

Scout Pins recognise meaningful contribution to the Guide.

They celebrate the different ways Scouts help the Guide grow.

Scout Pins are intended to tell the story of a Scout's journey rather than measure their status.

No two Scouts are expected to earn the same Pins.

No two Backpacks are expected to look alike.

---

# Philosophy

Scout Pins exist to recognise contribution.

They do not represent rank.

They do not grant authority.

They do not determine trust.

They do not create competition.

Instead, Scout Pins celebrate curiosity, stewardship and exploration.

Every Pin should answer one simple question:

> How has this Scout helped the Guide?

---

# Design Principles

Every Scout Pin should:

- recognise meaningful contribution
- encourage exploration
- celebrate quality over quantity
- reinforce the philosophy of the Guide

Scout Pins should never encourage:

- repetitive behaviour
- meaningless contribution
- popularity
- competition
- engagement for its own sake

---

# Categories

Scout Pins may recognise many different forms of contribution.

Examples include:

## Discovery

Recognising Scouts who expand the Guide.

Examples:

- discovering new products
- documenting new retailers
- expanding into new regions
- expanding into new countries

---

## Documentation

Recognising Scouts who improve the quality of the Guide.

Examples:

- product photography
- measurements
- packaging documentation
- barcode discovery

---

## Research

Recognising careful investigation.

Examples:

- historical research
- manufacturer research
- archival discoveries

---

## Stewardship

Recognising long-term care for the Guide.

Examples:

- improving existing documentation
- preserving historical information
- confirming community knowledge
- maintaining product quality

---

## Exploration

Recognising curiosity.

Examples:

- visiting new retailers
- documenting unfamiliar products
- broad geographical coverage

---

# Display

Scout Pins are displayed on the Backpack.

A Backpack should display only a small number of featured Pins.

Scouts choose which Pins to display.

This allows every Backpack to tell a different story.

Pins not currently displayed remain part of the Scout's collection.

---

# Awarding Pins

Scout Pins should be awarded automatically wherever practical.

Pins should recognise sustained meaningful contribution rather than isolated actions.

The exact criteria for individual Pins are intentionally defined separately from this document.

---

# Rarity

Pins should not be designed around rarity.

Some Pins will naturally become uncommon because they represent uncommon contributions.

Rarity should emerge from contribution rather than being artificially imposed.

---

# Future Expansion

The Scout Pin system is intended to grow alongside the Guide.

New forms of contribution may introduce new Pins without changing the underlying philosophy.

Existing Pins should remain meaningful over time.

---

# Summary

Scout Pins celebrate how a Scout has helped the Guide.

They are not rewards.

They are not ranks.

They are not status symbols.

They are reminders of journeys taken, discoveries made and knowledge preserved.

Every Backpack tells a different story.

Scout Pins help tell that story.
