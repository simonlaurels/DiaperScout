# Scouts

## Purpose

Scouts are Explorers who choose to help the Guide grow.

Every Scout brings knowledge back from the world so that future Explorers can discover products, retailers and experiences that would otherwise remain undocumented.

Scouts are the primary source of Community Observations within DiaperScout.

They do not define the Guide.

They help the Guide understand the world.

---

# Becoming a Scout

Every account begins as an Explorer.

Explorers may use the Guide without ever contributing.

When an Explorer's first contribution becomes part of the Guide they become a Scout.

Becoming a Scout is not a reward.

It marks the beginning of a different relationship with the Guide.

Explorers discover knowledge.

Scouts preserve it.

---

# Scout Callsigns

Every Scout chooses a unique Scout Callsign.

The Callsign becomes the Scout's public identity throughout DiaperScout.

Observations, discoveries and contributions are attributed to the Callsign rather than a real name.

A good Callsign should be:

- unique
- memorable
- respectful

Over time, a Callsign becomes associated with the Scout's contribution to the Guide.

---

# The Backpack

Every Scout carries a Backpack.

The Backpack is the Scout's public profile within DiaperScout.

Unlike a traditional social media profile, the Backpack exists to tell the story of a Scout's journey.

It reflects where a Scout has explored, what they have documented and how they have helped the Guide grow.

A Backpack may contain:

- Scout Callsign
- chosen Scout Pins
- notable discoveries
- Field Journal
- contribution highlights
- occasional impact summaries

The Backpack intentionally avoids:

- follower counts
- popularity metrics
- public trust scores
- engagement statistics
- leaderboards

The Backpack belongs to the Scout.

The Guide remains the main character.

---

# The Field Journal

Every accepted observation becomes part of a Scout's Field Journal.

The Field Journal is a chronological record of discoveries.

It provides provenance for observations while allowing Explorers to understand a Scout's journey through the world.

A Field Journal may contain:

- product discoveries
- retailer observations
- measurements
- photographs
- historical research
- other accepted Community Observations

Observations remain attributed to the Scout who recorded them.

Where corrections are necessary they are made transparently.

The history of an observation is preserved.

---

# Scout Pins

Scout Pins recognise meaningful contribution.

They do not represent rank.

They do not represent authority.

They do not create competition.

Instead they tell the story of how a Scout has helped the Guide.

Pins may recognise contributions such as:

- discovering new products
- documenting retailers
- measuring products
- photographing products
- preserving historical information
- expanding the Guide into new places

Scouts choose which Pins they display on their Backpack.

No two Backpacks need look the same.

---

# Recognition

Recognition celebrates contribution.

It does not reward activity for its own sake.

DiaperScout values:

- curiosity
- careful observation
- thoughtful documentation
- stewardship
- long-term contribution

A single important discovery may be more valuable than hundreds of routine observations.

Recognition exists to celebrate the Scout's contribution to the Guide rather than encourage competition between Scouts.

---

# Impact

Occasionally, the Guide may reflect on how a Scout's discoveries have helped others.

These summaries exist to acknowledge meaningful contribution rather than measure popularity.

Examples include:

- Explorers helped
- products documented
- retailers improved
- countries explored
- observations contributing to community knowledge

Impact summaries should be infrequent.

Their purpose is reflection rather than engagement.

---

# Privacy

DiaperScout respects the privacy of every Scout.

Real names are never required.

Scout Callsigns form the public identity of the community.

Internal trust remains private to the Guide.

Scouts should never feel that contributing knowledge requires revealing more personal information than necessary.

---

# Community

Scouts are collaborators.

Not competitors.

When Scouts encounter conflicting information, they gather more observations.

Evidence becomes stronger through continued discovery rather than debate.

Every Scout contributes a different piece of the world.

Together those discoveries allow the Guide to become richer, broader and more useful than any individual could create alone.

---

# Summary

Every Explorer begins with curiosity.

Some choose to become Scouts.

Scouts discover.

Scouts document.

Scouts preserve.

Every discovery becomes part of the Guide.

One observation.

One retailer.

One neighbourhood.

One journey.

At a time.
