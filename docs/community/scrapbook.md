# Explorer's Scrapbook

## Purpose

Every Explorer keeps a Scrapbook.

The Scrapbook is a personal record of discoveries, contributions and memorable moments throughout their journey with the Guide.

Rather than rewarding exploration through traditional achievement systems, the Scrapbook celebrates the experiences that have helped enrich the Guide.

Every Scrapbook becomes unique over time.

No two Explorers are expected to have the same pages.

---

# Philosophy

The Scrapbook exists to celebrate contribution.

It does not represent rank.

It does not grant authority.

It does not determine trust.

It does not create competition.

Instead, the Scrapbook captures moments that reflect curiosity, stewardship and exploration.

Every page should answer one simple question:

> What story has this Explorer added to the Guide?

---

# Design Principles

Every Scrapbook page should:

- celebrate meaningful contribution
- encourage exploration
- reward curiosity
- reinforce the philosophy of the Guide
- feel personal and memorable

The Scrapbook should never encourage:

- repetitive behaviour
- meaningless contribution
- popularity
- competition
- engagement for its own sake

---

# The Scrapbook

The Scrapbook is intended to resemble an explorer's journal assembled over many journeys.

Pages may include elements such as:

- handwritten notes
- maps
- sketches
- stamps
- stickers
- photographs
- date stamps
- pressed leaves
- washi tape
- tickets and keepsakes

The visual presentation should reinforce the feeling that every Explorer is documenting their own adventures.

---

# Types of Pages

The Scrapbook may contain many different kinds of pages.

Examples include:

## Discovery

Celebrating Explorers who expand the Guide.

Examples:

- discovering new products
- documenting new retailers
- expanding into new regions
- expanding into new countries

---

## Documentation

Celebrating improvements to the quality of the Guide.

Examples:

- product photography
- measurements
- packaging documentation
- barcode discovery

---

## Research

Celebrating careful investigation.

Examples:

- historical research
- manufacturer research
- archival discoveries

---

## Stewardship

Celebrating long-term care for the Guide.

Examples:

- improving existing documentation
- preserving historical information
- confirming community knowledge
- maintaining product quality

---

## Exploration

Celebrating curiosity.

Examples:

- visiting new retailers
- documenting unfamiliar products
- broad geographical exploration

---

## Community

Celebrating contributions that help fellow Explorers.

Examples:

- observations marked as helpful
- improving clarity
- welcoming new Explorers
- assisting with evidence

---

# Adding Pages

New pages should usually be added automatically when an Explorer reaches a meaningful milestone.

Pages should celebrate sustained contribution rather than isolated actions.

The precise criteria for individual pages are intentionally defined separately from this document.

---

# Rarity

Pages should never be designed around artificial rarity.

Some pages will naturally become uncommon because they reflect uncommon discoveries or contributions.

Their value comes from the story they tell rather than how many Explorers possess them.

---

# Relationship with the Backpack

The Scrapbook is one of the tools carried within an Explorer's Backpack.

The Backpack contains the resources that support an Explorer's journey.

The Scrapbook records the journey itself.

---

# Future Expansion

The Scrapbook is intended to grow alongside the Guide.

New kinds of discoveries and contributions may introduce additional pages without changing the underlying philosophy.

Older pages remain part of an Explorer's story.

Nothing is ever replaced.

The journey simply continues.

---

# Summary

The Explorer's Scrapbook celebrates how an Explorer has helped the Guide grow.

It is not a collection of achievements.

It is not a measure of status.

It is not a reward system.

It is a living record of discoveries made, knowledge preserved and stories worth remembering.

Every Scrapbook tells a different story.

That story belongs to the Explorer who created it.
