# Community Observations

## Overview

Not everything worth knowing about a product can be measured.

Some of the most valuable knowledge comes from people who have actually used it.

DiaperScout captures this knowledge through **Community Observations**.

Community Observations complement the Product Specification by recording real-world experiences while keeping objective product information separate.

Together they create a richer and more trustworthy explorer's guide.

---

# Product Specifications and Community Observations

DiaperScout intentionally separates objective facts from subjective experiences.

| Product Specification | Community Observation |
|----------------------|-----------------------|
| What is this product? | What is it like to use? |
| Objective | Subjective |
| Verifiable | Personal |
| Stable | Experience-based |
| Manufacturer-focused | Explorer-focused |

Neither is more important than the other.

They answer different questions.

---

# What Is a Community Observation?

A Community Observation is any contribution that describes an Explorer's experience, opinion or discovery relating to a product.

Observations may confirm expectations, highlight differences, or reveal information that cannot be captured by objective specifications alone.

An observation represents **one Explorer's perspective**.

Multiple observations together create a broader understanding of a product.

---

# Types of Community Observation

Community Observations may include, but are not limited to:

## Reviews

Long-form impressions after using a product.

Reviews often discuss:

- comfort
- absorbency
- confidence
- value
- overall satisfaction

---

## Ratings

Simple numerical or descriptive assessments.

Examples include:

- Overall rating
- Comfort
- Value for money
- Would buy again

Ratings should always be interpreted alongside written observations.

---

## Fit Notes

How a product fits in practice.

Examples:

- Runs small
- Fits high on the waist
- Narrow between the legs
- Better suited to taller wearers

These describe personal experience rather than objective measurements.

---

## Performance Observations

Experiences that cannot be reduced to a specification.

Examples include:

- Performed well overnight
- Leak guards worked effectively
- Tapes remained secure
- Plastic backing felt quieter than expected

Different Explorers may report different experiences.

---

## Comparisons

Comparisons between products.

Examples:

- Softer than BetterDry
- More discreet than Crinklz
- Fits similarly to Trest Elite

Comparisons provide valuable context but remain subjective.

---

## Availability Discoveries

Community knowledge about where products can be found.

Examples include:

- Recently stocked by a retailer
- No longer available locally
- Imported from another country

Retail information changes frequently and benefits from community contribution.

---

## Photographs

Explorer-submitted photographs showing products in real-world settings.

Examples include:

- Packaging
- Product details
- Prints
- Construction features
- Side-by-side comparisons

Photographs often reveal details missing from manufacturer imagery.

---

## Historical Notes

Observations about changes over time.

Examples include:

- Packaging redesign
- New print introduced
- Tape design changed
- Manufacturer updated materials

These observations help document the evolution of products.

---

# Why Keep Observations Separate?

Separating Product Specifications from Community Observations has several advantages.

## Product Specifications remain objective.

The factual description of a product never changes because opinions change.

---

## Community knowledge can evolve.

Explorers may disagree.

New discoveries may be made.

Products may behave differently under different circumstances.

Community Observations are designed to capture this diversity.

---

## Both become more trustworthy.

Objective information remains authoritative.

Subjective experiences remain authentic.

Neither compromises the other.

---

# Multiple Perspectives

No single observation represents absolute truth.

Different Explorers may report different experiences because of differences in:

- body shape
- usage
- expectations
- clothing
- environment
- personal preference

Rather than forcing a single conclusion, DiaperScout values multiple perspectives.

Patterns emerge naturally as observations accumulate.

---

# Writing Good Observations

Helpful observations are:

- honest
- respectful
- specific
- descriptive
- supported by experience

Helpful observations explain **why** an Explorer reached a conclusion.

For example:

> "The tapes remained secure after approximately ten hours."

is more useful than:

> "Great tapes."

Specific observations help future Explorers make informed decisions.

---

# Product Specifications Take Priority

Where an objective Product Specification exists, it should always be treated as authoritative.

Community Observations should not be used to replace factual product information.

For example:

- A photograph may suggest a product has changed.
- An observation may suggest different packaging.
- A review may mention additional features.

These discoveries should encourage verification and, where appropriate, updates to the Product Specification.

Community Observations enrich the Guide.

They do not redefine it.

---

# The Explorer's Notebook

One Explorer notices a quieter backing.

Another discovers a new retailer.

Someone else photographs a revised print.

Another compares sizing with an older product.

Individually these are observations.

Together they become collective knowledge.

Community Observations are the Explorer's notebook—the place where discoveries are shared, compared and preserved for everyone who follows.

---

# Summary

The Product Specification tells Explorers **what a product is**.

Community Observations tell Explorers **what it is like to experience that product**.

Keeping these distinct allows DiaperScout to remain both objective and deeply human.

Facts build trust.

Observations build understanding.

Together they create the Guide.
